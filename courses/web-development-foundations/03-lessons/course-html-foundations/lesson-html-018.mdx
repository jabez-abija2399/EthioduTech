<Context>
Writing HTML is only half the job. As developers, we must also be able to spot when our HTML makes a website difficult or impossible for some users to navigate.
</Context>

<Objective>
By the end of this lesson, you will identify structural accessibility violations including missing alt text and unlinked form labels.
</Objective>

<Concept>
## The Diagnosis Workflow
When auditing HTML for accessibility, ask yourself:
1. **Images**: Does every `<img>` have an `alt` attribute? Is the text meaningful, or is it empty (`alt=""`) for decorative images?
2. **Forms**: Does every `<input>` have a `<label>`? Is the `for` attribute matching the `id`?
3. **Semantics**: Is a `<div>` being used where a `<header>`, `<nav>`, or `<main>` would provide better structure?
4. **Headings**: Does the page have an `<h1>`? Do the headings skip levels (e.g., jumping from `<h1>` directly to `<h4>`)?
</Concept>

<InteractiveSandbox type="debugging_challenge">
Find the accessibility violation in this code:
```html
<main>
  <h2>Welcome to the Zoo</h2>
  <img src="lion.jpg">
  <p>Our lions are awake!</p>
</main>
```
</InteractiveSandbox>

<HintLadder>
- Level 1: Look at the image.
- Level 2: Screen readers cannot see the image. What attribute do they need?
- Level 3: The `<img>` tag is missing an `alt` attribute describing the lion.
</HintLadder>

<IndependentPractice>
Review the following form. Identify the two accessibility violations.
```html
<form>
  <label>Search:</label>
  <input type="text" id="query" name="q">
  <div class="submit-button">Go</div>
</form>
```
</IndependentPractice>

<Reflection>
Finding these issues *before* you publish your site ensures that everyone, regardless of how they browse the web, can interact with your content.
</Reflection>
