# Ethiopia Education Research

## FACT
- The Ethiopian education system is currently undergoing reforms, with a significant push towards digitization and digital literacy.
- Internet penetration in Ethiopia is growing but still limited outside major urban centers like Addis Ababa. Average speeds and connectivity reliability can be low.
- Smartphone usage is increasing rapidly among youth, though high-end devices are less common than affordable, lower-spec models.
- Low-bandwidth conditions are typical; high-definition video streaming is often unfeasible or too expensive for the average family.
- Mobile data can be expensive relative to average income, making offline or low-data capabilities critical.
- There is a high demand for digital skills (coding, web development) but limited access to high-quality, practical training centers, especially for younger students (Grades 5-12).

## ASSUMPTION
- Parents are willing to pay for educational platforms if they demonstrably improve their child's future employment prospects or practical skills.
- Students in Grades 5-12 have the cognitive capacity to learn programming fundamentals and web development if taught through project-based, interactive methods rather than traditional lectures.
- Local payment methods (e.g., Telebirr, CBE Birr) will be required to process subscriptions or course purchases locally.
- A significant portion of learning will happen on mobile devices (smartphones/tablets) rather than dedicated laptops, especially in early grades.

## HYPOTHESIS
- If we build a low-bandwidth, offline-capable progressive web app (PWA), we can reach students who are otherwise excluded by high-bandwidth video platforms.
- If we focus on project-based learning and portfolio generation, parents will see tangible value and be more likely to subscribe.
- A platform that requires students to actively code in the browser will result in higher retention and skill mastery compared to a platform that only offers video lessons and multiple-choice quizzes.

## DECISION
- The MVP will be a PWA optimized for low-bandwidth environments, favoring text, compressed assets, and local processing over heavy video streaming.
- The MVP will feature a built-in, browser-based code editor that works on mobile and desktop.
- The platform will integrate local Ethiopian payment gateways for the initial launch.
- The primary focus will be on tangible outputs (projects and portfolios) as proof of learning, replacing traditional certificates.
