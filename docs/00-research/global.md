# Global EdTech Research

## FACT
- Modern EdTech is shifting away from passive learning (watching videos) toward active learning (interactive coding environments, project-based tasks).
- Mastery learning—where students must demonstrate competence in one topic before advancing to the next—yields better long-term retention.
- Artificial Intelligence is increasingly used as a tutor or assistant (e.g., providing hints, explaining code errors) rather than just a grading tool.
- Version control (Git/GitHub) is a fundamental skill in professional software development, yet it is rarely taught effectively to younger students.
- Child safety regulations (e.g., COPPA, GDPR-K) require strict data privacy, minimal data collection, and parental controls for users under 13-16.

## ASSUMPTION
- Global learners, similar to Ethiopian learners, suffer from "tutorial hell"—watching tutorials without gaining the ability to build independently.
- An architecture that supports offline and low-bandwidth use in Ethiopia will also be highly beneficial in other developing markets (e.g., other parts of Africa, South Asia, Latin America).
- The project-based, portfolio-driven approach appeals universally to students and parents seeking practical career skills.
- AI tutors are most effective when they use the Socratic method (asking guiding questions) rather than providing direct answers.

## HYPOTHESIS
- By structuring courses around a "learning loop" (Diagnose → Learn → Practice → Build → Reflect), we will create more capable independent problem solvers than traditional LMS platforms.
- Integrating a simplified version control system early on will make students more comfortable with professional tools like GitHub later.
- Separating the "Learning Engine" from the "Content" will allow us to easily localize the curriculum for different regions and languages without rebuilding the platform.

## DECISION
- The platform will enforce a strict pedagogical model focused on active practice and building, rejecting the video-only course model.
- AI will be integrated specifically as a "learning assistant" that provides hints, asks questions, and reviews code, with safeguards to prevent it from simply giving away answers.
- The architecture will separate the core engine from content to support future international expansion and localization.
- Strict child safety and moderation tools will be built-in from the beginning to ensure a safe collaborative environment.
